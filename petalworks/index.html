The Story

About the Project

Inspiration

Endless short-video feeds have trained many of us to switch every few seconds. Focus timers try to help, but most either punish you (get distracted and your plant dies) or are forgettable (a number counting down). On a headset there's one more problem: people can't wear one for hours, and an app that treats taking it off as failure doesn't fit the device.

So I flipped it around. The headset is where you come back to, not where you have to stay. Plant a seed on your real desk, take the headset off, do your work, and come back to a flower blooming where you left it. Nothing ever dies.

What it does

  • Plant a seed in the planter on your desk and turn the dial, by touch or by look-and-pinch.
  • Focus with the headset on or off. The timer runs on the wall clock.
  • Come back to a growth replay, then say how it went: Done is a full bloom, Got pulled away a small bud. Honesty is never punished.
  • Your garden remembers: each flower stands for a kind of work, so a week of planters shows where your time went. Favourites go into a Showcase on your desk. Visitors arrive, species unlock with focus time, and streaks pause instead of resetting.
  • Florins are earned only by focusing, never bought, and spent on fantasy flowers. Longer stretches pay a little more per minute; a bud earns a flat 1 per minute, so honesty still pays.
Florins for an m-minute bloom f(m) = m × (1 + m / 60),  m ≤ 120 25 min → 35 florins  ·  50 min → 92 florins

No account, no ads, no data leaves the headset.

How I built it

Unity 6.3 LTS (URP) with Meta XR SDK v207 for Quest 3 / 3S:

  • Interaction SDK hand grab, poke and v207 gaze: every action works by touch and look-and-pinch, with no controllers.
  • MR Utility Kit finds real surfaces, with a fallback for unscanned rooms; spatial anchors keep the garden in place day after day.
  • Sessions are a stored UTC start time plus a duration, so headset off, focus loss or a killed app always recover. Saves are atomic.
  • Tested with EditMode tests, the Meta XR Simulator and XR Operator, then on a real headset every build. UI stays inside the 58° × 54° design area.

What I learned

Over 20+ builds in 18 days (v0.2.0 on Nov 1 to v0.4.x at submission), I logged 180+ pieces of playtest feedback, each with a fix:

  • Don't make people choose what they don't understand. A "Check-in / Stay-in" mode confused testers and changed nothing, so I replaced it with one line: "The timer keeps going if you take your headset off."
  • Show less, sooner. The planter comes first; everything else unlocks as you grow. The first session is locked to two minutes, so new users see a bloom right away.
  • A timer alone doesn't bring people back. One tester: "after a few days it's just a timer." That led to visitors, focus-paced unlocks and florins.

Challenges

  • Small targets in hand tracking: bigger drop zones and knobs, a tick for each notch, and seeds that fly back when dropped.
  • Unknown rooms: floor recovery, reset positions, and placement that needs only 22 × 22 cm of desk.
  • Solo in about three weeks: a strict cut line, and small changes checked on the headset.

What's next

Launching mid-December 2026 on the Meta Horizon Store, in time for New Year's resolutions. Free to start with an optional one-time unlock for more species and planter skins; florins are never sold. Then: seasons, custom activities, microgestures, voice-dictated seed labels and localization.

In the Headset

Hand Interactions

Touch It, or Look and Pinch

Petalworks is hands-only, end to end; a controller is never needed. It's built on the Meta Interaction SDK (v207), and every action works two ways: by direct touch, and by looking at it and pinching. That second path matters because the key objects are small (a seed, a dial knob) and it keeps the app usable seated, without reaching across the desk.

Plant

Pinch a seed from the tray and drop it into the pot. The drop zone is generous (18 × 18 cm, up to 15 cm above the rim) and glows while you carry a seed. Let go outside it and the seed flies back to its slot with a soft rustle, so nothing gets lost.

Set the time

Turn a physical dial on the planter. The knob is a chunky 3.6 cm cap you can grab anywhere, each notch clicks with a tick, and a knock confirms the time. It lets go the moment your hand opens or moves away, which fixed a playtest issue where a pinch didn't seem to release.

Press

Start, self-report and menus are real 3D press buttons you poke with a fingertip. When a fingertip or your gaze rests near a button, a small label tells you what it does before you press.

Arrange

Carry the planter, panels and garden by their edges and set them down on any surface. Pull a bloom out of the week planter and pot it into your Showcase.

Play

Touch a bee or butterfly, or look at it and pinch, and it does a little spiral, hop or flutter.

Designed from playtests

Every gesture was tuned across 20+ headset builds (v0.2.0 to v0.4.x in 18 days) and 180+ pieces of playtest feedback. First-run ghost hands demonstrate each gesture instead of text walls. An early palm-up hand menu kept triggering by accident, so I replaced it with a press button on the desk. One tricky bug: the garden grows in from 0.1% scale, and the SDK's poke buttons stored their press depth at that tiny size, so they stuck down after one press. I now re-measure them once the garden is full size.

Meta XR Capabilities

What It Runs On

Hands-first

No controller is ever needed. Every action works by direct touch (Interaction SDK hand grab and poke) and by look-and-pinch.

Gaze · ISDK v207

Seeds, the timer dial and its notches, the Journal, buttons and visitors all respond to gaze + pinch, so small targets stay easy to hit.

Hand Tracking

Grabbing seeds, turning the dial knob, carrying the planter by its edges, potting flowers into the Showcase, poking press buttons.

Passthrough

The whole experience happens on your real desk. The garden is part of your room, not a virtual scene.

MR Utility Kit

Finds real desks and surfaces to place the planter and garden, with a fallback for rooms without Space Setup: the planter floats in front of you and you set it down on any surface.

Spatial Anchors

The garden, planters and Showcase come back in the same spots on your desk, day after day.

FoV-aware Design

All essential UI sits inside the 58° × 54° design area, ready for glasses-class devices.

Focus-aware Lifecycle

The timer runs on a stored wall-clock start time, so taking the headset off, opening system windows or the app being killed never fails a session.

Simulator + XR Operator

Play-mode testing with synthetic hands, head pose and pinches, then a real headset pass on every build.

Why VR

Technology That Gives Attention Back

I've been working in VR for the last seven or eight years, and what excites me most right now is that it's finally starting to fit around our lives instead of asking us to leave them.

For most of that time the pitch was escape: put on a headset and go somewhere else. Mixed reality flips that. Your real desk, your real room, become the canvas, and hands and eyes make the interaction feel like touching things rather than operating software. Building Petalworks, the moment that sold me was a playtester's reaction to a little bee landing on a flower on their own desk: "Loved the bee, it's too cute!!!" Nobody had to explain it.

I'm also excited about the shift toward lighter devices and shorter sessions. The future I want isn't wearing a headset for eight hours; it's a calm place you check in with for a few minutes, that remembers where you left things, and that sends you back to your day a little better off. Persistent anchors, glasses-class hardware and designing for a narrower field of view all point that way.

After years of watching the medium grow, and at a time when so much tech is built to keep us scrolling, VR has a real chance to be the opposite: technology that gives people their attention back. That's what I want to keep building.
